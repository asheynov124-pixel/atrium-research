What controls specific jurisdiction here?

A forum may hear a claim when the defendant purposefully availed itself of the forum and the claim arises out of or relates to that contact. Relatedness does not require a strict causal chain.

Specific jurisdiction exists when the defendant purposefully avails itself of the forum and the suit arises out of or relates to those contacts. clean · Ford

A strict causal requirement is not required: a resident plaintiff injured in the forum by a product the defendant systematically markets there can proceed even if that unit was sold elsewhere. Ford, 141 S. Ct. at 1026–27

Unrelated claims by non-residents remain outside specific jurisdiction. Bristol-Myers, 137 S. Ct. at 1781 caution · distinguish plaintiffs

Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court

141 S. Ct. 1017 (2021) · public opinion text

The plaintiffs alleged injury in the forum from a Ford vehicle. Ford argued the particular cars were designed, made, and first sold elsewhere, so the claims did not arise from forum contacts. The Court held that the connection required is that the suit arise out of or relate to the defendant’s contacts with the forum. A strict causal relationship is not required. Ford had systematically served the market in Montana and Minnesota. That relatedness was enough.
